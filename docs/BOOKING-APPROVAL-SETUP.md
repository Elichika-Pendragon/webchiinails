## Retention and operating decisions still outstanding

The earlier release has no cleanup job; the latest revision deploys owner-authorized receipt-only daily cleanup at 07:15 WIB; first scheduled execution is unobserved. Seven-day/start-time expiry stops approval of a pending request; it does not erase stored names, WhatsApp numbers, quoted totals, payment-proof images, hashed references or audit data. Owner GET is read-only and can display effective expiry without writing it to the database.

The owner has chosen the receipt-image policy stated below; retention/deletion of remaining customer metadata is still undecided. Agree an appropriate privacy notice, retention period, deletion process, backup policy and credential-handling/rotation policy for routine customer use. A provisional option for review is deleting cancelled/expired requests 30 days after they become terminal, and separately agreeing retention for completed appointments. This option is not implemented or approved. This undecided metadata policy does not authorize metadata deletion or prevent the explicitly authorized resource setup, receipt-only cleanup and controlled integration test.

Never run blanket deletion against `approving`, `reconcile` or future accepted requests. Their claims protect against duplicates. An accepted request's deterministic event metadata and idempotency records may still be required to reconcile retries or edits. Any eventual cleanup must preserve unresolved cases and future reservations, define what happens to Calendar copies and backups, and be tested independently. Contact/payment details in Calendar require the same privacy decision as D1 data.

No request bodies, receipt bytes/base64, WhatsApp numbers, approval tokens, authentication cookies, private keys or upstream error bodies should be written to ordinary logs. Platform request/analytics retention must also be checked; approval URLs contain a token even though that token does not authorize owner access.

This design reduces Chii's daily work but is not fully maintenance-free. Quotas, credentials, platform changes, failed approvals and direct Calendar edits still require an accountable owner and a recovery process.
